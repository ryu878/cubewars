## Findings (actionable)

### F-001 (informational — NOT a blocking finding for EPIC 14; strongly recommend an urgent follow-up task)

- Checklist # / Gate: #16 (Playability sanity) / Tester's "test-harness note"
- Severity: **critical in effect, but out of scope for this EPIC's diff** (the affected file, `server/src/index.ts`, is not touched by `git diff e05f49e..HEAD`)
- Issue: Tester's report characterized the `MIN_INPUT_INTERVAL_MS` (15ms) shared per-connection debounce (`server/src/index.ts:26,170-181`) colliding with a back-to-back `input`+`fire` send as "a test-script issue, not a product bug." **I independently re-tested this against the server with a script that faithfully reproduces the ACTUAL client's real architecture** (an independent `setInterval` sending `input` every 33ms — exactly `client/src/main.ts`'s real `INPUT_SEND_INTERVAL_MS`, confirmed at `main.ts:360` — plus asynchronously-timed `fire` sends on a separate, uncorrelated clock, exactly matching `fpsController.ts:95-103`'s real click handler, which is not synchronized to the input loop in any way). Method and result:
  - **Control** (fire-only, no competing input stream, `public` mode with no bots to avoid a death confound): 100/100 fire attempts produced a `fire_broadcast` echo — 0% drop. Confirms the debounce itself is not spuriously dropping isolated fire attempts, and that my measurement harness is sound.
  - **Real-architecture simulation** (competing 33ms `input` timer + fire clicks on an independent, uncorrelated timer, gaps always ≥350ms so the separate weapon-cooldown limiter — 300ms for pistol — is never the cause): three independent runs, 200/120/200 attempts, produced **45.5%, 39.2%, and (an initial bots-mode run confounded by bot kills) 56.0%** silent-drop rates. The two clean (non-bot, no-death-confound) runs (45.5%, 39.2%) bracket the theoretical prediction almost exactly: with a uniformly-phased 33ms input clock and a 15ms blocking window after each accepted message, a click landing in that window (≈15/33 ≈ 45%) gets silently dropped with **no client-side indication whatsoever** — no ack, no retry, no error.
  - This means: in completely normal play — not flooding, not adversarial, just a real client's independent 30Hz input loop plus a human clicking to shoot — **roughly 40-55% of fire attempts never reach `resolveFire()` at all**, silently. The client has no way to detect this (the `onFire` callback in `fpsController.ts:100-103` already plays the muzzle flash, viewmodel recoil, AND (as of this EPIC) the gunshot sound unconditionally, before sending, with no feedback loop confirming the server actually processed it).
  - This is **pre-existing since EPIC 4** (confirmed via `git log --oneline | grep -i debounce/flood` and reading `docs/tasks/epic-04-critic-review.md` — the debounce's design intent was anti-flood/DoS protection specifically, and its regression testing at the time was "clear-LOS hit, wall-block, DPS cap, death/respawn, normal-play silence" — normal-rate legitimate input+fire collision at real human/client timing was apparently never isolated and measured). It is **not introduced or worsened in mechanism by this EPIC's diff** — but this EPIC does make the symptom more perceptually significant, since a confident audible "bang" now plays on every click regardless of whether the shot was ever attempted server-side.
- Required fix (file/behavior): **not required for this EPIC** — `server/src/index.ts` is untouched by this diff, and fixing this properly (e.g., a per-message-type debounce instead of one shared timestamp, or exempting `fire` from the generic floor since it already has its own weapon-cooldown gate, or reducing/removing the generic floor's interaction with `fire` specifically) is a genuine design decision that deserves its own scoped task and testing, not a drive-by patch inside a sound-system Critic review.
- Suggested check to prove closed (for the eventual follow-up task, not this one): re-run a variant of my reproduction script (or an equivalent) with the fix applied and confirm 0% (or near-0%) silent-drop rate under the same real-architecture simulation (competing 33ms input timer + independently-timed fire clicks), while re-confirming the anti-flood/DoS protection this debounce exists for (per `docs/tasks/epic-04-critic-review.md`'s Infosec note) is not weakened.
- **Recommendation to Team Lead**: file this as a new, high-priority task immediately — this affects the game's single most important verb ("shoot") for essentially every player, at a magnitude (~40-55%) far beyond noise, and is now more visible/misleading to players than before because of this EPIC's own (correct, well-built) audio feedback. I deliberately did not fix it myself or fold it into this EPIC's revision, since it requires a real design decision (which message-type debounce strategy) outside EPIC 14's spec and would be exactly the kind of unauthorized scope expansion `ai-office/agents/critic/role.mdc` prohibits.

## Notes (non-blocking)

- **Sound design quality, evaluated on the actual synth parameters, not the doc table's aspirational description** (read `client/src/audio/synth.ts` + `soundManager.ts` in full):
  - **Jump grunt** (`soundManager.ts:135-152`): bandpass-filtered noise (380Hz center, Q=0.9) layered with a sine tone ramping 220Hz→90Hz, 130ms. Noise-dominant + a genuinely low, breathy tonal component — reads as a human grunt, not a UI beep. Meets the explicit "must include a noise component" AC.
  - **Three gunshots**, checked for genuine escalation, not just "three different numbers": pistol = highpass noise (1800Hz cutoff, sharp/thin) + triangle tone 900→600Hz, 75ms, peakGain 0.3 (quietest, shortest). Rifle = bandpass noise (900Hz, Q=0.7, more body than pistol's highpass) + sawtooth tone 500→320Hz (harsher waveform than pistol's triangle), 115ms, peakGain 0.36 (louder). Grenade launcher = low sine 80→55Hz (peakGain 0.5, the loudest single component in the whole sound table) + lowpass noise (500Hz cutoff, dull/boomy) at 300ms — categorically different character (deep boom vs. crack) from the other two, not just "longer." This is a real escalation in filter type, waveform harshness, frequency register, gain, and duration simultaneously — matches `RECOIL_PROFILES`'s pistol<rifle<grenade_launcher escalation, independently cross-checked against `client/src/viewmodel.ts`'s existing recoil magnitudes.
  - **Three pickup categories**: weapon/ammo share a rising triangle-tone arpeggio (3-note vs. 2-note — a real, audible distinction, not a cosmetic doc-comment-only one); armor is a single square-wave tone (330Hz, buzzier/more "solid" than the sine-based tones elsewhere) + a short low-pass noise thump (a "clunk" edge); health is two overlapping sine tones (660Hz + 990Hz, a perfect fifth) with a noticeably gentler attack (20-30ms vs. armor's 6ms) — genuinely reads as "brighter/softer" vs. armor's "buzzy/solid." No two pickup categories share a waveform-type + noise-presence combination.
  - **Hit-taken** (`soundManager.ts:371-386`): sawtooth tone descending 480→180Hz (harsher waveform, higher starting pitch, and a different direction/register than the jump grunt's sine 220→90) + lowpass noise thump (420Hz cutoff, longer than footstep's 900Hz/55ms noise burst) — I checked this specifically against footstep and jump grunt for confusability (the EPIC's own explicit "must not be confusable" bar) and found real separation in filter type, waveform, frequency register, and duration on every axis, not just one.
  - One minor, non-blocking observation: the footstep sound (`playFootstep()`, `soundManager.ts:200-216`) is pure lowpass noise with no tonal "thump" layer, even though the spec's table said "possibly layered with a brief low-frequency thump" (explicitly optional wording) — a reasonable simplification, not a defect; it's still clearly distinct from every other sound in the table by virtue of being the only noise-only, no-tone, sub-60ms sound.
- **Footstep repetition-fatigue mitigation, independently confirmed real**: `FOOTSTEP_TIMING_JITTER_FRACTION = 0.075` and `FOOTSTEP_PITCH_JITTER_FRACTION = 0.08` (`soundManager.ts:67,75`) are applied via two **separate** `jitter()` calls (`Math.random()` invoked independently each time, `soundManager.ts:181,189,205`) — timing jitter recomputed once per cadence cycle, pitch jitter recomputed once per actual footstep play, so the two never correlate. Tester's captured live sample (intervals `[335,317,316,333]`ms, playback rates `[0.95,1.049,0.994,1.035,0.992]`) is consistent with genuinely independent randomization at the spec'd magnitude, not a fixed/looping pattern. This satisfies the brief's explicit "avoid robotic metronome" requirement, not just nominally.
